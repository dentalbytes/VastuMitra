Dining Room

The dining area is usually placed close to the kitchen for practical reasons, and Vastu guidance for it mostly concerns seating direction and keeping the space calm during meals.

Ideal Direction

The West part of the house is generally preferred for the dining room, ideally adjoining the kitchen. The head of the household is traditionally seated facing East while eating, with other family members facing East, West, or North.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Place the dining table in the West part of the house, near the kitchen.
  • Seat the family facing East, West, or North while eating.
  • Use a square or rectangular dining table rather than an irregular shape.
  • Hang a mirror on the wall facing the dining table (reflecting the food is considered to symbolize abundance).
  • Keep the dining area well lit and free of clutter.

Traditionally Avoided

  • Eating while facing South.
  • A dining table directly in line with the main entrance.
  • An irregularly shaped (round-cornered/oval mixed) or broken dining table.
  • Placing the dining area in the North-East corner of the house.
  • Eating directly in front of a mirror that reflects the diners themselves rather than the food.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

Core seating-direction guidance is broadly shared. A more visible regional difference is cultural rather than directional: many traditional South Indian households historically eat seated on the floor in a row (especially during festive or ceremonial meals, served on a banana leaf), which shapes how the dining space itself is laid out — often a cleared, flexible floor area rather than a fixed Western-style table — compared to the fixed dining-table arrangement more commonly assumed in North Indian Vastu guidance. The underlying facing-direction advice applies either way.
