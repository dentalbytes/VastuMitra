About & Disclaimer

What this site is

This is a reference guide to Vastu Shastra, the traditional Indian system of architecture and space planning. It organizes commonly cited Vastu principles by room — entrance, living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, pooja room, staircase, study, dining room, and balcony/garden — along with a companion guide to plants and trees traditionally considered auspicious or inauspicious near a home.

A traditional practice, not a scientific one

Vastu Shastra is a traditional and cultural belief system, rooted in ancient Indian texts such as the Sthapatya Veda, and passed down through generations of practitioners, family custom, and regional lineage. It is not an empirically validated science — its claims about how directional placement affects health, wealth, or wellbeing have not been established through the scientific method, and independent researchers have generally found no controlled evidence supporting a causal link between room orientation and life outcomes.

That doesn't mean the guidance here is meaningless. Many Vastu principles overlap with genuinely sound architectural common sense — good cross-ventilation, morning sunlight in living spaces, keeping fire and water sources apart in a kitchen, and reducing clutter near entrances are all reasonable design choices on their own merits, independent of any belief system.

We'd encourage you to treat this content as a window into a rich cultural and architectural tradition, and as one input among many — alongside your own needs, your budget, local building codes, and (for anything structural) a licensed architect or engineer — rather than as a set of scientifically proven rules.

Why we cover both North and South Indian traditions

Vastu Shastra isn't a single, unified rulebook — it has developed differently across regions, family lineages, and textual traditions over centuries. Rather than presenting one regional interpretation as the single "correct" version, this guide follows the core principles that both North and South Indian traditions broadly share, and calls out — in the highlighted boxes on each page — the specific points where regional practice tends to diverge. Where we weren't confident a claimed difference was well established, we've tried to say so plainly rather than overstate it.

A living document

Vastu is a vast subject with real disagreement even among practitioners of the same tradition. This guide aims to reflect commonly cited, mainstream principles rather than every possible variation, exception, or remedy. If your family or community follows a different custom than what's described here, that's expected — local tradition is just as valid a source as any written text.
