Kitchen

The kitchen is governed by the fire element (Agni) in Vastu, and is one of the areas where directional guidance is most consistent across regional traditions — the South-East corner is almost universally favored.

Ideal Direction

The South-East (Agneya) corner of the home is considered the ideal location for the kitchen, since it is ruled by Agni, the fire deity. Where South-East isn't available, North-West is generally offered as the next best alternative. The stove itself is traditionally placed so the person cooking faces East while cooking.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Position the stove/cooktop so the cook faces East (some traditions also accept North).
  • Keep a gap between the stove (fire) and the sink or water storage (water) — the two elements are kept apart.
  • Place the water source and sink in the North-East or East part of the kitchen.
  • Store grains, food, and utensils in the South or West part of the kitchen.
  • Keep the kitchen well ventilated with a window or exhaust, ideally on the East wall.

Traditionally Avoided

  • A kitchen located in the North-East corner of the house, considered highly unfavorable.
  • The stove and sink placed directly next to or facing each other.
  • Cooking while facing South.
  • A kitchen directly below a bathroom, or sharing a wall with the pooja room.
  • A dark, poorly ventilated kitchen with no natural light.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

This is one of the areas of strongest agreement between traditions — South-East placement and an East-facing cook are widely accepted in both North and South Indian Vastu literature. Minor regional differences tend to show up around kitchen customs rather than core directional rules: for example, some South Indian households traditionally treat the kitchen as a more ritually protected space (with customs around entering it after a bath, or before touching other parts of the house), which can shape kitchen placement relative to bathrooms and entrances in practice, even though the underlying South-East rule itself is shared.
