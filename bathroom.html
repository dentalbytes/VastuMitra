Bathroom & Toilet

Because bathrooms combine water and waste, Vastu treats their placement carefully — mainly in terms of which corners of the house to keep them away from, more than a single "ideal" spot.

Ideal Direction

The North-West or West part of the house is generally preferred for bathrooms. The toilet seat itself is traditionally oriented so the user faces North or South while seated, never East or West.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Place bathrooms in the North-West or West zone of the home.
  • Position the toilet seat facing North or South.
  • Keep the bathroom well ventilated with an exhaust fan or window.
  • Keep the door closed and the toilet lid down when not in use.
  • Use light colors and keep the space clean and dry.

Traditionally Avoided

  • A bathroom in the North-East corner — considered one of the least favorable placements in the whole house.
  • A bathroom directly above or below the kitchen or pooja room.
  • A toilet seat facing East or West.
  • A bathroom door directly facing the main entrance or the kitchen door.
  • Leaks, dripping taps, or standing water left unattended.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

Avoiding the North-East for bathrooms is one of the most consistently repeated rules across both regional traditions. A practical difference shows up in older versus newer homes: many traditional South Indian homes historically kept bathrooms as a small detached structure outside the main house altogether (a practice tied to older sanitation customs), which sidesteps a lot of the internal-placement questions modern apartment-style Vastu guidance has to deal with. In contemporary construction across both regions, though, the North-West/West placement guidance is applied in essentially the same way.
