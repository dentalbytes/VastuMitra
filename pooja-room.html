Pooja Room

The prayer or pooja room is considered the most sacred space in the home, and its placement guidance is among the most uniform across every regional Vastu tradition.

Ideal Direction

The North-East (Ishaan) corner is universally regarded as the best location for a pooja room, since this direction is associated with divine energy and spiritual purity. Idols or images are typically placed facing East or West, with the person praying facing East or North — never South.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Place the pooja room or altar in the North-East corner of the house.
  • Face idols/images East or West; face East or North yourself while praying.
  • Keep idols slightly away from the wall, not touching it directly.
  • Use light, sattvic colors like white, cream, or light yellow.
  • Keep the space clean, quiet, and separate from bedrooms and bathrooms.

Traditionally Avoided

  • A pooja room under a staircase or directly below/above a bathroom or bedroom.
  • Praying while facing South.
  • Broken idols, images, or damaged religious items kept on display.
  • A pooja room that shares a wall with a bathroom or is located inside a bedroom (though a curtained corner is sometimes accepted where space is limited).
  • Storing unrelated household items in the same space.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

North-East placement and East/North-facing worship are agreed upon almost universally. The visible difference between regions is more about the pooja room's form than its direction: South Indian homes (especially in Tamil Nadu and Kerala) often build a dedicated small shrine room or even a separate structure with its own threshold and sometimes a small dome, treated almost like a mini temple, whereas North Indian homes more commonly use a decorated wooden mandir unit or a wall-mounted shelf within a larger room. The underlying North-East and facing-direction rules are the same either way.
