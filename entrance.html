Main Entrance

In Vastu Shastra, the main entrance is treated as the point where energy first enters the home, so its direction, size, and condition are considered especially important — more so than almost any other single element of the house.

Ideal Direction

North, East, and North-East (Ishaan) facing entrances are traditionally considered the most favorable, as these directions are associated with the rising sun and the deities said to govern those quarters. A North-East entrance is often held up as the single most auspicious option where the plot allows for it.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Make the main door the largest and most decorative door in the house.
  • Keep the threshold clean and well lit, often marked with rangoli or a toran (door hanging).
  • Use door hinges that open inward smoothly and don't creak.
  • Place a nameplate or auspicious symbol (like Om or Swastik) near the entrance.
  • Keep the area immediately outside the door open, uncluttered, and well maintained.

Traditionally Avoided

  • A main door directly in line with the back door or a window (said to let energy pass straight through).
  • Broken door frames, squeaky hinges, or a door that sticks and doesn't open fully.
  • Placing a staircase, mirror, or toilet directly facing the entrance.
  • Dustbins, dead plants, or clutter piled near the threshold.
  • A South-facing main entrance without any of the traditional correctives.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

The broad preference for North, East, and North-East entrances is shared across both traditions. Where practice tends to diverge is in the level of detail: many South Indian Vastu practitioners (drawing on Kerala and Tamil Shilpa Shastra texts) further subdivide each wall into 32 units, or padas, to fine-tune exactly where along that wall the door should sit — not just which of the four sides it's on. North Indian practice more commonly works at the level of the eight to nine broad compass directions. Local family tradition often matters as much as either written system, so treat this as a general guide rather than a strict rulebook.
