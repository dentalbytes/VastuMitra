Bedroom

Bedrooms are considered spaces of rest and stability, so Vastu emphasizes heavier, grounded directions for them — particularly the South-West, associated with steadiness and strength.

Ideal Direction

The South-West (Nairutya) corner of the house is traditionally reserved for the master bedroom, since this direction is associated with the earth element, stability, and the strength of the household. Children's or guest bedrooms are often placed in the West or North-West instead.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Place the bed so the headboard is against the South or West wall.
  • Sleep with the head pointing South or East (South is often considered the most restful).
  • Leave equal, open space on both sides of the bed where possible.
  • Keep the room's heaviest furniture in the South or South-West.
  • Use calming colors and ensure the room is well ventilated.

Traditionally Avoided

  • Sleeping with the head pointing North — widely discouraged across nearly all Vastu traditions.
  • A mirror positioned to reflect the bed.
  • A bed placed directly beneath an exposed ceiling beam.
  • A bedroom located in the North-East corner of the house.
  • Attached bathrooms with the toilet aligned directly with the bed.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

The South-West master bedroom rule and the advice against sleeping with the head to the North are remarkably consistent across regional traditions — the reasoning given (that the human body's electromagnetic field is said to conflict with the Earth's magnetic field in that orientation) is cited in both North and South Indian sources. Where practice diverges more is around which family member occupies which bedroom: some South Indian family traditions place stronger emphasis on the eldest family member or head of household occupying the South-West room specifically, tied to broader joint-family household customs, while North Indian guidance more often frames it simply as "the primary bedroom" without that specific emphasis.
