Balcony & Garden

Open spaces — balconies, terraces, courtyards, and gardens — are treated as important "breathing" zones in Vastu, and are also where most of the plant and tree guidance actually gets applied.

Ideal Direction

Open space, gardens, and lighter landscaping are best kept in the North, East, or North-East, where they allow in morning light without obstruction. Heavier landscaping, taller trees, or a garden shed are better suited to the South or West, where their height and weight are considered more "grounding."

Traditionally Recommended

  • Keep the North and East open, with only low plants or none at all, so light isn't blocked.
  • Place taller trees or heavier landscaping in the South or West.
  • Keep balconies and terraces clean, uncluttered, and free of stagnant water.
  • Use the North-East corner of a garden or terrace for a small water feature if desired.
  • Choose auspicious plants (see the full plants guide) for balcony pots.

Traditionally Avoided

  • Tall trees or heavy structures in the North or East that block sunlight.
  • Overgrown, unmaintained, or dead plants left on a balcony or in a garden.
  • Thorny or milk-sap plants kept close to the main entrance.
  • Water stagnation in pots, drains, or garden features.
  • A large tree planted directly in front of the main door, blocking the entrance.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

Keeping the North-East light and open is agreed upon everywhere. The clearest regional difference here is which plants and trees carry special significance: coconut and banana plants hold particularly strong ritual and architectural importance in South Indian tradition (both are central to ceremonies and are commonly grown near the home), while Neem, Peepal, and Tulsi are emphasized more universally but especially in North Indian household custom.
