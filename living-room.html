Living Room

The living room (or drawing room) is where guests are welcomed, so Vastu treats it as a space that should feel open, well lit, and positioned to receive positive energy from the entrance.

Ideal Direction

The North, East, or North-East zone of the house is generally preferred for the living room, since it benefits from morning light and is considered energetically favorable for social spaces. In many home layouts, the living room is placed close to the main entrance for exactly this reason.

Traditionally Recommended

  • Seat guests facing East or North, and seat the head of household facing East or North while entertaining.
  • Place heavier furniture (sofas, cabinets) along the South or West walls.
  • Keep the North-East corner of the room open and lightly furnished.
  • Use light, warm colors and ensure good natural light and ventilation.
  • Hang artwork depicting nature, sunrises, or serene scenes rather than conflict or distress.

Traditionally Avoided

  • Heavy furniture or clutter in the North-East corner, blocking the "energy" of the entrance.
  • Mirrors placed directly opposite the main seating, reflecting people while they sit.
  • Broken furniture, clocks that don't work, or dying plants left in the room.
  • A living room tucked into the South-West corner of the home, traditionally reserved for the master bedroom.

North Indian vs. South Indian view

Both traditions broadly agree that living and reception spaces belong in the North, East, or North-East of the plot. A more regional difference shows up in home layout style: traditional South Indian homes (particularly Kerala's nalukettu style) often organize living spaces around a central open courtyard (nadumuttam), treating the courtyard itself as the most auspicious point of the house — a layout idea less commonly emphasized in North Indian residential Vastu, which tends to focus more on direction-wise room placement within a conventional floor plan.
